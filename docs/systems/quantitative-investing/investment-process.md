# The quantitative investment process

## Intuition

A useful forecast is only one component of a profitable strategy. An investor
also needs a risk model, a way to size positions, executable trades and feedback
from realised outcomes. The draft's preface and chapter 1 organise the subject
around this connected process.

## Mechanics from the draft

The investment opportunity depends on instruments, venues and participants.
Exchanges, dealers and brokers perform different roles; hedgers, index funds,
active managers and informed traders have different objectives and constraints.
Possible sources of excess return include arbitrage, compensation for risk or
illiquidity, financing constraints, predictable flows and superior information.
These categories overlap (§§1.1–1.4).

The process has three stages (§1.5):

| Stage | Main question | Outputs |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Before the trade | What can be predicted, at what uncertainty and cost? | Dated data, return forecasts, risk and cost estimates |
| During the trade | What should be held and traded now? | Positions, hedges, constraints and orders |
| After the trade | What happened and what should change? | PnL attribution, model evaluation and capital allocation |

Risk research supports alpha research, and portfolio construction can change
the realised value of the same alpha. Performance attribution then informs
both research and sizing. Treating each component as an isolated modelling
competition misses these interactions.

## Complementary guidance

Describe a strategy with its universe, decision times, holding horizon,
funding/reporting currency, tradable instruments and cost model. A claim about
an opportunity should identify who supplies the opposing trade and why the
opportunity survives costs and competition.

Keep four quantities separate: predicted return, expected executable return
after costs, predicted risk, and realised PnL. A risk factor is not necessarily
a profitable signal. A statistically predictive signal may have no deployable
capacity. Retain data and configuration identity so a change in realised
performance can be traced to a model, universe or execution change.

## Limits and sources

The draft uses volatility as its central risk measure. Liquidity, tail losses,
financing, counterparty and operational risk require additional modelling.
Its workflow is a useful organising principle, not proof that a strategy has
an edge.
